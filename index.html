PV–Battery Islanded Microgrid Dynamic Performance Study

Objectives:

This project presents a simulation-based dynamic performance study of an islanded microgrid integrating photovoltaic (PV) generation and battery energy storage systems (BESS). The objective is to analyze frequency stability, power sharing behavior, and disturbance response in inverter-dominated microgrids operating without a utility grid connection. The system is implemented in MATLAB/Simulink, where:

  • PV systems operate using MPPT-based grid-following control

  • Battery inverters operate as grid-forming units using droop control

  • Secondary frequency control restores nominal frequency after disturbances

A new topology for loadability of power transmission lines.

Overview:

The growing demand for electrical energy has made the loadability of transmission lines a critical issue, as more power plants are built to meet rising consumption needs. However, transmission lines have limited capacity, which poses a challenge for delivering the generated power. Since loadability is influenced by voltage levels and line impedance, this thesis work explores enhancing it by reducing line impedance through frequency variation. Specifically, it investigates the use of cycloconverter-based frequency converters to adjust the transmission frequency and thereby improve loadability.

Challenges:

  • Increase the power transmission capacity of existing lines with minimal changes to it's infrastructures.

  • Support the theory with computer simulation and make reports on it.

  • Find the limitation of the proposed theory and define future work scopes.

Outcome:

The research work was concluded with the following outcome:

  • Possible to increase the loadability by reducing the frequency of transmitted power keeping the generation and load frequencies intact.

  • Frequency reduction can be achieved using cycloconverter based frequency converters.

  • No major changes required in power transmission scheme except the installation of frequency converter banks at the sending and receiving ends of the lines.

Future work:

Although, we made a successful simulation, the thesis comes with limitations. It needs high capacity cycloconverters to handle such voltages and currents that can escalate the costs and make this theory impractical. So, practical implementation of this topology is yet to be verified. I wrote a manuscript (unpublished) on my thesis work that you can check out to get more idea about my work.

Load flow study of a 7-bus system using PSAF

Objectives:

In this task, load flow study for a 7-bus system was performed using CYMEPSAF software. The performance of a power system in steady state condition is of primary importance for utility agencies. Given a specified load condition, the basic question to be answered are:

  • What are the line and transformer loads throughout the system?

  • What are the voltages throughout the system?

We are particularly interested in answering these questions as we evaluate proposed changes in an existing system such as:

  • New generation sites

  • Projected load growth

  • New transmission line

Load flow solution of a system provides these essential information

Outcome:

From the report of CYMEPSAF, voltage magnitude and voltage angle of the buses, power flow through the lines, power losses etc. were known. You had to identify different abnormal conditions (overloaded and underloaded lines and transformers, buses outside voltage limit etc.) from the results. You had to determine what to do in order to ameliorate these conditions. For example, we added a capacitor bank (static VAR compensator) at the buses to improve the bus voltages. We also added additional lines to improve overloaded line conditions. Additional lines also improve the bus voltages.

Implementation of a power factor improvement plant using MATLAB

Objectives:

For fixed power and voltage, the load current is inversely proportional to the power factor. The lower the power factor, the higher the load current and vice-versa. At lower power factor the kVA rating of the equipment has to be increased, making the equipment larger and expensive. Moreover, if power is distributed at low power factor, line loss increases and also conductor size has to be increased for the higher current. Therefore, low power factor is not allowed in supply systems. The low power factor is mainly due to the fact that most of the industrial loads are inductive and therefore take lagging currents. In order to improve the power factor, some elements taking lagging current should be connected in parallel with the load. One of such elements could be capacitance. In this project, we implemented a power factor improvement plant with capacitor banks and show its effectiveness using MATLAB.

Methodology:

By installing suitably sized switched capacitors into the circuit, the Power Factor is improved and the value becomes nearer to 1 thus minimising wasted energy and improving the efficiency of a plant. So, we create a MATLAB code that basically simulates the power factor improvement method using parallel capacitors. For a given value of input of inductance parameters of the circuit, the code produces necessary capacitances to improve the power factor to unity and 0.98 respectively. It also produces the total current before and after the correction. The complete project report and MATLAB code are attached below. Feel free to check them out.

Combined Cycle Power Plant Output Prediction Using ANN

Overview:

This project uses an Artificial Neural Network (ANN) to predict the net hourly electrical energy output (MW) of a combined cycle power plant. By using key environmental and operational features, the model provides accurate power output predictions. The model achieves an R² score of 93.56% on the test data.

Dataset:

The dataset used is from the UCI Machine Learning Repository and includes 9568 rows of data with the following features:

  • Temperature (°C)

  • Exhaust Vacuum (mm Hg)

  • Ambient Pressure (bar)

  • Relative Humidity (%)

  • The target variable is the Energy Output (MW)

The dataset is publicly available at: UCI Repository - Combined Cycle Power Plant Dataset.

Model Architecture:

The ANN model consists of:

  • Input Layer: 4 nodes (one for each feature)

  • Hidden Layers: 2 layers with ReLU activation functions

  • Output Layer: 1 node with a linear activation function for continuous output

The model demonstrates strong accuracy in predicting the power output of the plant using environmental parameters with an MSE score of 17.95%.

24-Hour & Multi-Day Power Demand Forecasting - LSTM

Overview:

This project uses LSTM-based Seq2Seq models to forecast short-term (next 24 hours) and long-term (30 days) electricity demand for the Bangladesh Power Grid. The model is trained on real demand data from Power Grid Company of Bangladesh (PGCB).

  • Forecast horizon: 24 hours (single-day) and 30 days (multi-step rolling).

  • Based on hourly demand data from PGCB.

  • Model: stacked LSTM layers with dropout and dense layers for final regression.

  • Trained using MSE loss with Adam optimizer and learning rate scheduling.

Prediction:

After completing data cleaning, preprocessing, and sampling, the model was evaluated in two test scenarios. In the first case, it was trained to predict the next 24 hours of demand using the previous seven days of data from the training set. In the second case, one week of data from 21–28 August 2025 was used to forecast the demand for 29 August 2025. The predicted values were then compared with the actual demand data provided by PGCB.-

SoC-Aware Droop Control of a PV–Battery Islanded AC Microgrid

2025 - 2026

Independent Research

  • Proposes an SoC-adaptive droop mechanism for heterogeneous BESS units in AC microgrids to address unequal energy depletion—an issue not handled by conventional droop methods.
  • Developing a detailed 9-bus microgrid model in MATLAB/Simulink with heterogeneous GFM/GFL inverter architectures, incorporating realistic line impedances to evaluate power-sharing performance under non-ideal network conditions.
  • Designing adaptive droop coefficients to dynamically regulate active power dispatch based on battery SoC for balanced utilization across asymmetric storage units.
  • Preliminary validation of the core GFM/GFL droop architecture on a simplified lossless 5-bus model prototype (130 kW PV + 110 kW BESS): frequency nadir 49.67 Hz under 80% load step, battery sharing error \textbf{< 5\%}, secondary frequency restoration to 49.999 Hz within 2.4 s.
